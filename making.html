1934 – 1950

Making of the Constitution

A Constitution written by Indians, for India — drafted in the shadow of Partition, debated for 165 days, and adopted on 26 November 1949. This is how the world's longest written constitution came to be.

Timeline

Key milestones

The demand for a Constituent Assembly grew through the 1930s and 1940s; the Assembly itself sat for 2 years, 11 months and 18 days.

1934

First proposal

M.N. Roy, a pioneer of the Communist movement in India, proposes the idea of a Constituent Assembly to frame India's Constitution — the first such demand.

1935

The demand is adopted

The Indian National Congress officially demands a Constituent Assembly. The Government of India Act, 1935 introduces provincial autonomy — the constitutional backdrop for the debate.

1938

Nehru's declaration

Jawaharlal Nehru declares: 'The Constitution of free India must be framed, without outside interference, by a Constituent Assembly elected on the basis of adult franchise.'

1940

The August Offer

The British government recognises the demand for a Constituent Assembly in principle for the first time.

1942

Cripps Mission

Sir Stafford Cripps proposes a constitution-making body elected by provincial legislatures — the framework that eventually shaped the Assembly (elections later held in 1946).

March 1946

Cabinet Mission Plan

The Cabinet Mission (Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander) proposes a Constituent Assembly of 389 members — 292 from British India and 93 from princely States — elected indirectly by provincial legislatures.

July 1946

Assembly elections

The Congress wins a large majority; the Muslim League wins most Muslim seats and then boycotts the Assembly, demanding Pakistan.

9 December 1946

First sitting

The Constituent Assembly meets for the first time at the Constitution Hall, New Delhi. Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha, the oldest member, presides as interim chairman. 211 members attend.

11 December 1946

Rajendra Prasad elected President

The Assembly elects Dr. Rajendra Prasad as its permanent President; H.C. Mookherjee is Vice-President (V.T. Krishnamachari later).

13 December 1946

Objectives Resolution

Jawaharlal Nehru moves the historic Objectives Resolution, setting out the ideals the Constitution would embody. Adopted on 22 January 1947, it became the basis of the Preamble.

3 June 1947

The Partition decision

The Mountbatten Plan is announced; a separate Constituent Assembly is created for Pakistan, shrinking the Indian Assembly to 299 members.

15 August 1947

Independence — and a sovereign Assembly

India becomes independent. The Constituent Assembly becomes a fully sovereign body, its members also acting as the Dominion's Parliament.

29 August 1947

Drafting Committee

A seven-member Drafting Committee is constituted under Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, working from the draft prepared with the Assembly's constitutional adviser, B.N. Rau.

4 November 1948

Draft introduced

Dr. Ambedkar moves the draft Constitution in the Assembly (first reading). The clause-by-clause consideration runs from 15 November 1948 — 114 days of debate on the draft.

26 November 1949

Adoption

The Constitution is adopted and the Preamble enacted. It contains 395 articles, 22 parts and 8 schedules. The calligraphed copies (art by Nandalal Bose's studio, calligraphy by Prem Behari Narain Raizada) are signed by 284 members on 24 November 1949.

26 January 1950

The Republic is born

The Constitution comes into force. The Constituent Assembly becomes the Provisional Parliament. Dr. Rajendra Prasad is sworn in as the first President of India. The date honours the Purna Swaraj resolution of 26 January 1930.

The Framers

Key figures

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Chairman of the Drafting Committee, chief architect of the Constitution. Led the drafting of the text, championed the Fundamental Rights and social justice provisions, and piloted the draft through the Assembly. Called the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'.

Dr. Rajendra Prasad

President of the Constituent Assembly. Presided over its sessions, later became the first President of India (1950–62).

Jawaharlal Nehru

Moved the Objectives Resolution that formed the philosophical basis of the Constitution; chaired the Union Powers and Union Constitution Committees.

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

Chairman of the Provincial Constitution Committee and the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal Areas. Shaped the federal and minority-protection provisions.

B.N. Rau

Constitutional Adviser to the Assembly. Produced the initial draft (1947) after studying constitutions worldwide, on which the Drafting Committee worked.

S.N. Mukherjee

Chief Draftsman of the Constitution and a member of the Drafting Committee's support team, who gave the final draft its precise form.

Drafting Committee members

Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadulla, N. Madhava Rau and T.T. Krishnamachari — besides the chairman, Dr. Ambedkar.

Women of the Assembly

15 women members including Sarojini Naidu, Rajkumari Amrit Kaur, Hansa Mehta, Durgabai Deshmukh, Sucheta Kripalani, Begum Aizaz Rasul, Renuka Ray, Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit, Ammu Swaminathan and Dakshayani Velayudhan.

Other notable members

Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha (interim chairman), Sir C. Rajagopalachari, Dr. S. Radhakrishnan, Frank Anthony, J.B. Kripalani, H.C. Mookherjee, T. Prakasam, Biswanath Das and John Mathai.

Inspiration

What was borrowed, from where

Dr. Ambedkar: 'The Constitution of India is a bag of borrowings.' Its framers adapted the best of existing constitutions to Indian conditions.

SourceFeatures adopted
United KingdomParliamentary system, rule of law, single citizenship, bicameral legislature, cabinet system, writs, parliamentary privileges, legislative procedure
United StatesFundamental Rights, judicial review, independence of the judiciary, impeachment of the President, office of the Vice-President, removal of judges
IrelandDirective Principles of State Policy, nomination of members to the Rajya Sabha, method of election of the President
CanadaFederation with a strong Centre, residuary powers with the Centre, appointment of Governors by the Centre, advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court
AustraliaConcurrent List, freedom of trade and commerce, joint sittings of the two Houses
Weimar GermanySuspension of Fundamental Rights during an Emergency
Soviet UnionFundamental Duties (added 1976), ideal of justice — social, economic and political — in the Preamble
FranceRepublic, ideals of liberty, equality and fraternity
South AfricaProcedure for amendment of the Constitution, election of Rajya Sabha members
Japan'Procedure established by law' (Article 21)
Not borrowed: the Indian Constitution kept features distinctly its own — universal adult franchise from day one, a Union (not a federation of independent states), Directive Principles alongside Fundamental Rights, and an independent Election Commission with superintendence over elections.

Did you know? The original Constitution was not printed but handwritten in English, calligraphed by Prem Behari Narain Raizada, with each page decorated by artists from Shantiniketan under Nandalal Bose. A Hindi version was later calligraphed and illustrated by Nandalal Bose's team. The originals are kept in helium-filled cases in the Parliament Library.
