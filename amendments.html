Constitutional Amendments

A constitution that cannot be amended becomes brittle; one that can be amended too easily becomes a plaything of the majority. Article 368 strikes the balance — and these 106 amendments, enacted since 1951, are the record of that balance in action.

106Amendments enacted
1976The 42nd — the 'mini-Constitution'
2016The 101st brought GST
How amendments are made (Article 368): most amendments need a special majority in each House — a majority of the total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting. Amendments touching the federal structure, the judiciary, elections or representation of States also need ratification by at least half the State legislatures. Fewer than 20 of the 106 amendments required State ratification. And since Kesavananda Bharati (1973), no amendment may destroy the Constitution's basic structure.
